## February 2026 Sampling

### Overview

This protocol describes the collection, fixation, processing, embedding, sectioning, and staining of fish gonads for histological analysis. The samples were collected and processed during February 2026 along the Israeli Mediterranean coast.

The main purpose of this protocol is to prepare fish gonad tissue for microscopic examination in order to identify reproductive condition and gonadal developmental stage. The protocol focuses on two species: ***Diplodus sargus*** and ***Pagrus caeruleostictus***.

### Objectives

The objectives of the protocol were:

- To identify gonadal developmental stages.
- To detect transitional gonads.
- To prepare clear histological sections that can be used for reliable interpretation.
- To evaluate which stages or tissue types are more sensitive to tearing during processing.

### Species Studied

The protocol was applied to the following fish species:

| Species | Common group | Sampling month | Tissue examined | Main analysis goal | Notes |
|---------|--------------|----------------|-----------------|--------------------|-------|
| *Diplodus sargus* | Seabream | February 2026 | Gonads | Developmental stage | Histology and egg counting |
| *Pagrus caeruleostictus* | Seabream | February 2026 | Gonads | Developmental stage | Histology and egg counting |

## Sampling and Dissection

Fish were collected and processed throughout February 2026. During dissection, the following measurements were recorded:

- Total length.
- Total weight.
- Gonad weight.
- Otolith extraction for age determination.

The right gonad was selected for histology to maintain consistency between samples. In some cases, this choice was changed when tissue quality required it. The left gonad was collected for egg counting when eggs were present and was preserved in 70% ethanol. The decision to use the right gonad for histology and the left gonad for egg counting was arbitrary, but it helped create a consistent workflow.

<u>Consistency in gonad selection is important because it reduces variation between samples and makes comparison between individuals easier.</u>

## Fixation and Storage

### Fixation in Davidson's Solution

Gonads were fixed in **Davidson's solution**. Fixation time was adjusted according to tissue type and maturity:

- Immature or female tissue: **24-30 hours**.
- Mature male tissue: **approximately 48 hours**.

Mature male gonads were fixed for a longer period because they were larger and less structurally stable compared with female gonads or immature gonads.

### Transfer to Ethanol

After fixation, samples were transferred to 70% ethanol and stored at room temperature.

## Tissue Processing

### Dehydration

Samples were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, followed by butanol:

| Step | Solution | Concentration | Duration | Temperature | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------------|----------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Ethanol | 70% | 4 days | Room temperature | Storage and initial dehydration |
| 2 | Ethanol | 95% | 30 hours | Room temperature | Continued dehydration |
| 3 | Ethanol | 100% | 48 hours | Room temperature | Complete dehydration |
| 4 | Butanol | 100% | 4 days | Room temperature | Transition before paraffin |
| 5 | Paraffin bath 1 | Paraffin | 22 hours | 60 degrees C | Tissue infiltration |
| 6 | Paraffin bath 2 | Paraffin | 21 hours | 60 degrees C | Final infiltration before embedding |

### Paraffin Embedding

Samples were embedded in paraffin at 60 degrees C. The first paraffin bath lasted 22 hours, and the second paraffin bath lasted 21 hours. After infiltration, the samples were embedded in paraffin blocks.

*The trimming and alignment of the samples were performed after paraffin infiltration, which made it more difficult to identify the correct orientation for embedding the samples in the paraffin blocks. In future procedures, trimming and alignment should be performed immediately after fixation and before transfer to 70% ethanol.*

## Microtome Sectioning

Paraffin blocks were stored in a refrigerator for 24 hours and then placed in an ice bath for 1 hour before sectioning. Cooling improved the cutting quality, but some sections still showed folding and tearing.

Early-stage female gonads were especially fragile and often disintegrated during sectioning. This suggests that tissue maturity and structure strongly affect section quality.

## Histological Staining

### Modified Masson's Trichrome

Slides were stained using a modified **Masson's Trichrome** protocol. The staining sequence included:

- Hematoxylin.
- Bouin's solution.
- Acid Fuchsin.
- Phosphomolybdic acid.
- Aniline Blue.
- Acetic acid.

This staining method helped distinguish tissue structures and made the gonadal developmental stage easier to interpret under the microscope. An online chemical staining protocol related to this method is available here: [Sigma-Aldrich Trichrome Stain (Masson) Kit, Procedure No. HT15](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/deepweb/assets/sigmaaldrich/product/documents/425/788/procedure-no-ht15-trichrome-stains-masson-91873-ls-7-0-ms.pdf).

## Key Insights

The main insights from this protocol were:

- Fixation time must be adjusted according to tissue type and gonad maturity.
- Sample freshness is critical.
- Early-stage gonads are fragile.
- Cooling the paraffin blocks before sectioning improves cutting quality.
- Orientation should be planned before paraffin infiltration.

Overall, the protocol was successful. Most histological sections were clear and allowed identification of the gonadal developmental stage. Further investigation is needed to understand why female gonads at early developmental stages appeared torn and less distinct in the histological sections.
